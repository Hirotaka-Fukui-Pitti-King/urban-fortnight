# Statistical Mechanics in Optimizing Heterogeneous-Agent General Equilibrium

## Central question

**Can statistical-mechanical methods and mechanisms coexist with optimizing heterogeneous agents, rational expectations, and all relevant general-equilibrium market-clearing conditions?**

This question **precedes** New Keynesian macroeconomics. The project first builds a benchmark incomplete-market general equilibrium, studies the compatibility of each econophysics mechanism in isolation, and then introduces Calvo nominal rigidity and monetary policy only for compatible variants.

### Economic definition of success

An additional process is **not** an optimizing rational-expectations equilibrium merely because a stationary histogram or fixed point exists. Every endogenous transition must be generated by a specified optimal or equilibrium strategy, or else be explicitly classified as a heuristic comparison. Relevant markets must clear, individual budgets and firm/bank accounts must balance, and rational forecasts must agree with the aggregate law of motion.

In particular:
- **Yakovenko/kinetic exchange:** exogenous random money swaps are not automatically voluntary, optimal trades; derive a bilateral choice or matching protocol and its collision operator.
- **Ising:** logit choice can arise from optimized binary choice with payoff complementarities and specified taste shocks; an arbitrary expectation heuristic does not automatically preserve rational expectations.
- **Entropy:** the invariant distribution of a GE model need not maximize entropy under a convenient set of moments; prove the necessary conditions or record the failure.
- **Bouchaud:** a rule-of-thumb Mark-0 firm simulation is useful for comparison, but its rules are not silently rebranded as an optimal firm problem.
- **Challet/Minority Game:** strategic Nash/RE trading and adaptive score-updating can represent different solution concepts; test both without conflating them.
- **Random matrices:** initially a diagnostic for endogenous security-return correlations, not a separate economic microfoundation.
- **ABM:** a particle implementation of the same optimal policies and market clearing is a numerical representation, not a behavioral assumption.

## Stage 0–1 computed results (November 3, 2026)

The stationary Aiyagari model has now been implemented and executed in Python. With beta=0.96, alpha=0.36, delta=0.08, 360 asset points and 5 income states, the locally verified solution has capital K=5.706221, net interest rate r=0.038097, wage w=1.198024, asset Gini=0.493914, and relative capital-market residual 2.31e-8. Six Stage 0 tests passed; five numerical-grid and shock-grid specifications were solved.

Stage 1 simulates the same optimal policy functions at fixed equilibrium prices. In three Monte Carlo seeds with N=40,000, mean capital was 5.669039 versus deterministic K=5.706221. This is a preliminary finite-agent comparison and not a finite-N re-cleared general equilibrium. The results, CSV reference files, and executable scripts are located in Stage 0–1 folders; GitHub Actions additionally uploads complete generated distributions, policies, SVG figures and PDFs as build artifacts.

Stage 2 now constructs a restricted optimizing bilateral service-for-token economy with an exact kinetic collision operator, voluntary participation, productive home services, mass and token conservation, and stationary rational continuation values. The capital-producing Aiyagari benchmark remains in a separate block; complete nonseparable capital-money general equilibrium, bargaining price determination and monetary equilibrium selection remain open. The distinction between money entropy and Foley's transaction entropy is documented in Stage 2.

## Stage 2-B: integrated equilibrium attempt (2026-11-03)

A new Python test jointly chooses capital and money portfolios and bargains endogenously over a finite token-payment and service-quantity menu. At the computed fixed-price candidate, Bellman residual is 9.66e-9 and the DM collision operator conserves money and capital. **The full general equilibrium is NOT obtained:** capital excess -3.8801, money excess +0.1976, labor excess -0.0539, goods residual +0.5831. Portfolio taste shocks also induce money demand without meetings. Results and code in [Stage 2-B](research/02-kinetic-exchange-yakovenko/stage2b/README.md). Critically, Molico and Zhang (2006) already examine money and capital portfolio distributions, so future work should build directly on that model rather than advertise joint optimization as new.

## Stage 2-C — Canonical Molico--Zhang reconstruction, not yet monetary GE (2026-11-03)

New branch `research/stage2c-canonical-monetary-ge` and Draft PR #6. The November 2005 original primary paper shows type-specific capital used in decentralized production and a centralized capital-money market with **no production**. This contradicts any claim that Stage 2-B's representative-firm CM was a Molico--Zhang replication. A distinct Python finite-grid equation-level pilot implemented original equations (1)–(14) and all Table 1 primitives; seven local economic tests passed. However, price scans (5×5 grids p_k=.5,1.4,2; 7×7 grid p_k=1.4) found **no verified positive-price stationary equilibrium** with normalized fiat supply=1. Mean money holdings were approximately 0, 0, 0.3338, and an unconverged 0.5262 respectively. Research status remains OPEN, source/numerical limitations and economic accounting are in Stage 2-C/notes. Next gate: endogenous fiat-money clearing inside the joint private-value, bargaining and stationary-measure iteration.

Stage 2-C multistart evidence: at the same price 3, a zero-value initial condition yields an inactive private stationary state (mean money 0.34306), whereas a positive-value concentrated initial condition yields a converged actively trading private state (capital 0.98658, money 1.18318, trade 0.24136). Both fail nominal money supply=1 and are **not** monetary general equilibria. This invalidates a single-init price clearing procedure; continued work must solve positive monetary branch and price clearing jointly.

## Stage 2-D — CM money auction repair, full stationary equilibrium still open

A new Stage 2-D Python model repairs inconsistent wealth-grid interpolation in the old Bellman-versus-distribution policies (old active branch sup difference 356.426, 181 changed Nash contracts), and allows sellers to receive money above the grid ceiling for *next-period* holdings. Endogenous conditional CM price clearing with an optimal indifference lottery is verified at 5x5, 7x7 and 9x9 grids (prices 3.110784, 3.053066, 2.979536, next money exactly 1). However the joint stationary price/value/distribution iteration fails: **Stage 2-D is not a stationary monetary general equilibrium or original-study replication**.
